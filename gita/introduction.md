# Bhagavad Gita: Introduction

An overview of the whole Gita: its setting, speakers, structure of the 18 chapters, and the key verses to know first.

## The Basics

- **Where it sits:** inside the *Mahabharata*, in the Bhishma Parva, on the battlefield of Kurukshetra, just before the war begins.
- **The setup:** Arjuna sees his own family, teachers and friends on the opposing side, collapses emotionally, drops his bow and refuses to fight. Krishna, his charioteer, spends the next 700 verses restoring his clarity and resolve.
- **Narrative frame:** a story within a story. Sanjaya narrates the battlefield to the blind king Dhritarashtra, who asks the opening question in verse 1.1.
- **Speakers (commonly cited count):** Krishna ~574, Arjuna ~84, Sanjaya ~41, Dhritarashtra 1.

## The 18 Chapters

Traditionally divided into three sets of six chapters.

### Part 1: Karma (how to act), Chapters 1-6

| # | Chapter | Verses | Core idea |
|---|---------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | Arjuna Vishada Yoga | 47 | Arjuna's breakdown and despair |
| 2 | Sankhya Yoga | 72 | The Self is eternal; do your duty; the steady-minded person (*sthitaprajna*). Often called the Gita in summary |
| 3 | Karma Yoga | 43 | Action is unavoidable, so act without selfishness |
| 4 | Jnana Karma Sannyasa Yoga | 42 | Krishna's avatars; knowledge that purifies action |
| 5 | Karma Sannyasa Yoga | 29 | Renunciation vs. action: act, but stay unattached |
| 6 | Dhyana Yoga | 47 | Meditation; taming the restless mind |

### Part 2: Bhakti (devotion and surrender), Chapters 7-12

| # | Chapter | Verses | Core idea |
|---|---------|--------|-----------|
| 7 | Jnana Vijnana Yoga | 30 | Knowing the Divine both in theory and in experience |
| 8 | Akshara Brahma Yoga | 28 | The imperishable; what happens at death |
| 9 | Raja Vidya Raja Guhya Yoga | 34 | The "royal secret": devotion; even a leaf offered with love is accepted |
| 10 | Vibhuti Yoga | 42 | Krishna's glories: "among X, I am the best of X" |
| 11 | Vishvarupa Darshana Yoga | 55 | The Universal Form; Arjuna sees everything and is overwhelmed |
| 12 | Bhakti Yoga | 20 | Shortest chapter: the qualities of a true devotee |

### Part 3: Jnana (knowledge of what is real), Chapters 13-18

| # | Chapter | Verses | Core idea |
|---|---------|--------|-----------|
| 13 | Kshetra Kshetrajna Vibhaga Yoga | 34 | The field (body) vs. the knower of the field (Self) |
| 14 | Gunatraya Vibhaga Yoga | 27 | The three gunas: sattva, rajas, tamas |
| 15 | Purushottama Yoga | 20 | The inverted ashvattha tree; the Supreme Person |
| 16 | Daivasura Sampad Vibhaga Yoga | 24 | Divine vs. demonic traits |
| 17 | Shraddhatraya Vibhaga Yoga | 28 | Three kinds of faith, food, charity and austerity |
| 18 | Moksha Sannyasa Yoga | 78 | Longest chapter: the grand summary and final surrender |

**Total: 700 verses.** Some editions include an extra opening verse in Chapter 13 (Arjuna's question), giving 701.

## Five Verses to Know First

- **2.47** *karmaṇy evādhikāras te...* You have a right to your action, never to its results.
- **2.20** The Self is never born and never dies.
- **4.7-8** *yadā yadā hi dharmasya...* Whenever dharma declines, I manifest Myself.
- **6.5** *uddhared ātmanātmānaṁ...* Lift yourself by your own self; do not let yourself sink.
- **18.66** *sarva-dharmān parityajya...* Abandon all dharmas and surrender to Me alone; do not grieve.

## How It Ends

**नष्टो मोहः स्मृतिर्लब्धा** (*naṣṭo mohaḥ smṛtir labdhā*), Gita 18.73

*My delusion is destroyed; I have regained my memory (of who I am).*

**In simple words:** these are Arjuna's last words in the Gita. After 700 verses, the confused man picks up his bow again with a clear mind. The point of studying the Gita is not to escape one's battles, but to step into them with clarity.
